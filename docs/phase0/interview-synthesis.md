## Synthesis

**Desk-research problem statement (not interview-validated):** Ugandan SMEs and exporters with real orders cannot reliably finance the gap between buying/processing goods and being paid, because bank finance is short-term, expensive and collateral-heavy; Uganda's capital markets remain largely untapped for this segment despite rapid growth.

**Three publicly documented financing pain points** (regulator/institution sources, not anonymized interview quotes):

1. **Working-capital gap before payment.** An exporter with a UGX 10bn European order must fund coffee purchase, processing, packaging and shipping months before payment arrives. Source: [CMA, *Beyond Coffee and Gold*, 6 Jul 2026](https://cmauganda.co.ug/2026/07/06/beyond-coffee-and-gold-how-capital-markets-can-power-ugandas-export-revolution/).
2. **Collateral and cost barriers at banks.** Bank loans are described as short-term, expensive and requiring substantial collateral, making sufficient financing difficult for SMEs. Same CMA source.
3. **Missing patient long-term capital for value addition.** Moving from raw to processed exports needs investment that banks alone cannot supply; agriculture employs ~72% of the labour force and is 24.8% of GDP, while IFC priorities centre on agribusiness, financial inclusion and manufacturing. Sources: [CMA, *Unlocking Long-Term Financing Potential*, 15 Jul 2026](https://cmauganda.co.ug/2026/07/15/capital-markets-unlocking-ugandas-long-term-financing-potential-for-sustainable-economic-transformation/); [World Bank, Uganda overview](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/uganda/overview).

**Willingness-to-use proxy indicators (not participant findings):** domestic market capitalisation grew UGX 4.33tn → UGX 21.77tn (Jun 2021–Mar 2026); CIS AUM UGX 731bn → UGX 6.02tn; funded investor accounts ~29,000 → >220,000; MTN and Airtel IPOs succeeded. Source: CMA, 15 Jul 2026 (above). These show market capacity, not verified SME/investor intent.
